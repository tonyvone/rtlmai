\title{Neural Intelligence States: Exactly Mergeable Evidence\\
Replaces Trained-Adapter Averaging for\\
Compute-Efficient Distributed Adaptation}

\begin{abstract}
Distributed adaptation of foundation models today means independently
training low-rank adapters and averaging them---an approach that suffers
from geometric misalignment between per-client subspaces, pays a full
optimization run per model variant, and offers neither exact deletion nor
forgetting-free incremental updates. We propose \emph{neural intelligence
states} (RTLM-N): instead of communicating trained weights, every node
accumulates \emph{sufficient-statistic evidence} for adaptation
coordinates in a globally shared canonical basis anchored to a frozen
backbone. States merge by addition---exactly, in any order---and models
are \emph{materialized} from the merged state by regularized linear
solves. We show how to choose the shared basis (the optimal linear
correction has rank at most the output dimension, spanned by a
feature--residual cross-covariance computable on public calibration
data), how to reach nonlinear internal surfaces via distributed iterated
Gauss--Newton with per-round exact merging and measured linearization
error, and how the same algebra yields three properties no
trajectory-based method offers together: learning cost proportional to
\emph{new} data, zero forgetting, and exact machine unlearning by
subtraction. On real text classification with genuinely Adam-trained
competitors, canonical states score $0.750$ versus $0.538$ for federated
LoRA under severe non-IID skew at $46\times$ less measured compute;
1{,}000 model variants materialize from one state at $215\times$ less
measured compute than per-variant training; iterated Gauss--Newton
reaches within $4.5$ points of full backpropagation on an internal-only
adaptation task from a $48$-coordinate mergeable subspace; and a learning
inference cascade cuts energy per task $2.6\times$ within a single
workload as teacher escalations decline. Every result carries an explicit
guarantee class, and every number is reproducible from the released code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The economics of adapting foundation models are dominated by a structural
choice: learning is stored in \emph{trained weights}, produced by
optimization trajectories over data. This choice taxes every downstream
need. Producing $M$ tenant- or domain-specialized variants costs $M$
training runs. Keeping a deployed model current under drift costs either
a replay over all accumulated history or an incremental fine-tune that
forgets. Honoring a deletion request costs a retraining campaign or an
approximation. Federating adaptation across data silos costs quality:
independently trained low-rank adapters live in misaligned subspaces, and
averaging them interferes destructively.

We revisit an older statistical idea---sufficient statistics---in the
setting of frozen deep representations, and develop it into a complete
distributed learning system. The central object is a \emph{neural
intelligence state}: a compact set of Gram and cross-moment accumulators,
expressed in a canonical coordinate system shared by all nodes, from
which models are \emph{solved} rather than trained. Because the state is
a sum, its algebra is exact: merging is associative, commutative, and
order-free; deleting a contributor is a subtraction; and materialization
depends only on the totals, never on how many raw examples produced them
or in what order they arrived.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Canonical evidence states.} We replace
  independently-optimized adapters with per-node sufficient statistics
  for coordinates in a globally fixed adaptation basis
  ($\Delta\bW=\mathbf{U}\bC\mathbf{V}^\top$ with $\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}$
  shared), and prove exact centralized equivalence for frozen-feature
  surfaces under arbitrary partitions, merge orders, and deletions
  (Prop.~\ref{prop:exact}; measured deviation $\sim\!10^{-10}$ on real
  features).
  \item \textbf{Principled basis selection.} We show the optimal linear
  correction has rank at most the output dimension and characterize its
  input subspace via a feature--residual cross-covariance
  (Prop.~\ref{prop:subspace}), yielding a data-aware shared basis
  computable from a small public calibration set. This choice is
  decisive in practice: random shared bases lose to learned-subspace
  LoRA, while cross-covariance bases match or beat it.
  \item \textbf{Distributed iterated Gauss--Newton.} To adapt
  \emph{internal} transformer surfaces beyond the one-shot linearization
  band, we iterate: re-linearize at the current coordinates, accumulate
  fresh Jacobian evidence, merge exactly within the round, and take a
  Levenberg--Marquardt damped step, reporting the measured linearization
  error every round. States from different linearization points are
  never merged---a claim-discipline rule enforced by construction.
  \item \textbf{The incrementality trio.} The state algebra provides,
  simultaneously: learning cost $O(\text{new data})$ per refresh, zero
  forgetting (bit-identical to full retraining at every step), and exact
  unlearning by subtraction. We measure all three against replay
  retraining and warm-start SGD under distribution drift.
  \item \textbf{A learning inference cascade.} Escalations to a large
  verified teacher are absorbed into the state as residual evidence, so
  the escalation rate is a declining curve rather than a routing
  constant; we measure declining teacher usage and energy per task at
  stable quality, and project fleet-scale energy with all assumptions
  declared.
\end{enumerate}

Everything is released: a NumPy reference implementation, a PyTorch
backbone with exact autograd Jacobians, sixteen machine-checked
experiments, and per-claim guarantee labels
(\textsc{exact-frozen} / \textsc{linearized-bounded} /
\textsc{sketched-bounded} / \textsc{empirical-neural} /
\textsc{unsupported}).

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Federated learning and adapter merging.} FedAvg
\citep{mcmahan2017} averages locally trained weights; federated variants
of LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} inherit the misalignment problem of averaging
independently learned low-rank factors, as do post-hoc merging methods
such as task arithmetic \citep{ilharco2023}, model soups
\citep{wortsman2022}, and Fisher-weighted averaging
\citep{matena2022}. Our approach differs in kind: nodes never train or
transmit weights; they transmit evidence in a shared coordinate system,
and the server solves. One-shot federated learning via distillation
\citep{guha2019} shares the single-round goal but not the exactness.

\textbf{Machine unlearning.} Exact unlearning generally requires
retraining or sharded architectures \citep{bourtoule2021}; certified
removal for linear models \citep{guo2020} is the closest antecedent to
our deletion property. We extend exact removal to a fully distributed,
mergeable, multi-surface setting with lineage and audit.

\textbf{Continual learning.} Regularization methods such as EWC
\citep{kirkpatrick2017} mitigate but do not eliminate forgetting. For
supported surfaces our updates are exactly equivalent to retraining on
the union of all data, so forgetting is structurally impossible rather
than penalized.

\textbf{Linearized and second-order methods.} Our internal-surface
machinery builds on Gauss--Newton and natural-gradient approximations
\citep{martens2015} and on the linearization perspective of the NTK
\citep{jacot2018}; the trust-region damping is classical
Levenberg--Marquardt \citep{marquardt1963}. The novelty is the
distributed sufficient-statistic formulation with exact per-round
merging and enforced per-round error reporting.

\textbf{Cascades, distillation, and efficiency.} Knowledge distillation
\citep{hinton2015} transfers teacher behavior once; inference cascades
route between models. Our cascade \emph{accumulates}: escalations become
permanent state, addressing the energy concerns raised for large-scale
ML \citep{strubell2019,schwartz2020,patterson2021}. Linear probes on
frozen representations \citep{alain2017} underpin why the exact regime
is practically useful and should become more so as backbones improve.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Canonical evidence states}
Let a frozen backbone produce representations $\bh=f_\theta(x)\in
\mathbb{R}^d$, and let adaptation live in a globally shared basis:
head updates $\bW=\bW_0+\mathbf{U}\bC\mathbf{V}^\top$ with
$\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times r_u}$,
$\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r_v}$ fixed and identical on every
node, or generally $\Delta\theta=\mathbf{P}c$. For frozen-feature
surfaces node $i$ accumulates
\begin{equation}
\bH_i=\textstyle\sum_j \bh_j\bh_j^\top,\qquad
\bR_i=\textstyle\sum_j \bh_j r_j^\top,
\end{equation}
where $r_j$ is a target, teacher residual, or verified outcome; for
low-rank surfaces the same moments in coordinates
$z=\mathbf{V}^\top\bh$, $e_U=\mathbf{U}^\top(y-\bW_0\bh)$. The global
state is the sum over nodes, and materialization is a ridge solve,
$\bW^\ast=(\bH+\lambda I)^{-1}\bR$. States carry a content hash, an HMAC
signature, contributor lineage (for duplicate rejection), event
commitments (for audited deletion), and a guarantee class; merging
incompatible bases, backbones, or objectives is refused.

\begin{proposition}[Exactness and invariance]\label{prop:exact}
For a declared frozen representation and ridge objective, materialization
from the merged state equals centralized training on the pooled raw data,
for every partition of the data across nodes and every merge order; and
removing a contributor's state yields exactly the centralized solution on
the retained data.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\bH,\bR$ are sums of per-example terms, so any partition and order
yields the same totals; the strictly convex objective has a unique
minimizer depending only on those totals. Removal subtracts exactly the
removed examples' terms.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Choosing the shared basis}\label{sec:basis}
The choice of $\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}$ decides whether the constrained
space can express the needed correction.

\begin{proposition}[Optimal correction subspace]\label{prop:subspace}
Let $\Delta\bW^\ast=\arg\min_{\Delta\bW}\sum_j\|y_j-(\bW_0+\Delta\bW)
\bh_j\|^2+\lambda\|\Delta\bW\|_F^2$. Then
$\Delta\bW^\ast=(\bH+\lambda I)^{-1}\bC_{xy}$ with
$\bC_{xy}=\sum_j\bh_j e_j^\top$, $e_j=y_j-\bW_0\bh_j$; in particular
$\operatorname{rank}(\Delta\bW^\ast)\le k$ and its input subspace is
spanned by the columns of $(\bH+\lambda I)^{-1}\bC_{xy}$.
\end{proposition}

Thus a rank-$k$ shared input basis suffices in principle. We estimate it
from a small \emph{public} calibration set: leading directions from the
SVD of $\bC_{xy}$ (a preconditioner-free surrogate), padded with top
activation-variance directions and orthonormalized. Empirically this is
the difference between losing and winning against learned-subspace LoRA
(\S\ref{sec:exp-fed}).

\subsection{Internal surfaces: distributed iterated Gauss--Newton}
For an internal surface (e.g., a transformer MLP weight,
$\Delta\bW_1=\mathbf{U}\bC\mathbf{V}^\top$), the network is linearized in
the canonical coordinates: with
$\bJ(x)=\partial f/\partial c$ (computed exactly by autograd), nodes
accumulate $\bG_i=\sum\bJ^\top\bJ$, $\bg_i=\sum\bJ^\top r$, and the
merged state yields $c^\ast=(\bG+\lambda I)^{-1}\bg$
(\textsc{linearized-bounded}, with the linearization error measured on a
probe set and attached to the artifact). One-shot solves are confined to
a band around the reference model. We therefore iterate: at round $t$,
every node re-linearizes at the current $c_t$, fresh evidence is
accumulated and merged exactly \emph{within the round}, and a damped step
is taken with $\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}=\lambda+\mu\,\mathrm{tr}(\bG)/
\dim(c)$ (Levenberg--Marquardt). Rounds are distinct objectives; states
from different linearization points are never merged. Communication per
round is one compact state per node.

\subsection{Materialization, accretion, and migration}
\textbf{Factory.} One merged state materializes arbitrarily many
variants---regularization sweeps, tenant or geography slices via the
state algebra, contributor exclusions, reduced-rank power-budget
variants (exact sub-solves in leading canonical coordinates), and
quantized models (guarantee demoted accordingly)---with no access to raw
data and no representation passes.
\textbf{Cascade.} A small edge model answers first; a verifier gates on
confidence; failures escalate to a large verified teacher whose response
is folded into the state as residual evidence; the correction
rematerializes periodically.
\textbf{Migration.} When the backbone changes, a linear representation
bridge fit on public anchors transports heads
($\bW'=\mathbf{B}\bW$) or states
($\bH'=\mathbf{B}^\top\bH\mathbf{B}$); transported objects are labeled
\textsc{empirical-neural}, carry the measured bridge residual, and are
never claimed exact.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

\textbf{Setup and scope.} We evaluate on AG News \citep{zhang2015}
(120k real news texts, 4 classes) with transformer backbones
MLM-pretrained on the task corpus and then frozen (the build environment
cannot reach pretrained-checkpoint hosts; the released code includes a
HuggingFace wrapper exposing identical interfaces). All baselines that
claim SGD are genuinely trained with Adam, including backpropagation
through the backbone for internal LoRA. Compute is measured process
CPU-seconds; energy is CPU-seconds at a declared wattage (RAPL where
exposed). A NumPy reference program reproduces every algebraic claim
independently of PyTorch. We report the guarantee class with every
number.

\subsection{Exactness on real representations}
Across 10 nodes under severe label skew (Dirichlet $\alpha{=}0.1$),
arbitrary merge orders, node removal, and event deletion, merged-state
materialization matches centralized ridge training to a maximum
deviation of $2\times10^{-10}$ on real MLM features
(\textsc{exact-frozen}); state size is $O(d^2)$, independent of the
number of examples ($29\times$ smaller than the raw data in the
reference configuration, and constant as data grows).

\subsection{Canonical states vs.\ federated adaptation}\label{sec:exp-fed}
Eight non-IID nodes ($\alpha{=}0.1$), identical frozen features for all
methods, one-hot targets, accuracy on a held-out test set:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Method & Accuracy & Measured cpu-s \\
\midrule
Base head (400 labels) & 0.663 & --- \\
Federated LoRA (Adam, per-node subspaces, averaged) & 0.538 & 4.67 \\
One-shot FedAvg heads (Adam, averaged) & 0.557 & 3.97 \\
Centralized LoRA (Adam, all data) & 0.742 & 1.78 \\
\textbf{Canonical evidence state (merged, solved)} & \textbf{0.750} & \textbf{0.10} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The shared basis matters: with random shared directions the canonical
state scores only $0.462$--$0.475$; with the cross-covariance basis of
\S\ref{sec:basis} it beats even centralized SGD LoRA at $46\times$ less
measured compute, while remaining one-shot, mergeable, and deletable. In
a controlled reference experiment where the true correction lies in the
canonical space, averaging adapters trained in per-node misaligned bases
scores $0.745$ versus $0.990$ for canonical merging---the geometric
interference failure reproduced and then removed.

\subsection{One state, one thousand models}
From a single merged state we materialize $1{,}000$ variants
(regularization sweeps $\times$ contributor exclusions $\times$
quantization) in $7.8$ measured cpu-s with zero representation passes,
versus $\sim\!1{,}675$ cpu-s for per-variant SGD training:
$\mathbf{215\times}$ (\textsc{exact-frozen}). Excluded-contributor
variants are bit-identical to retraining without that contributor.

\subsection{Internal adaptation: iterated Gauss--Newton vs.\ backprop}
Internal MLP adapter only, head fixed, six non-IID nodes:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Method & Accuracy \\
\midrule
Fixed head, no adapter (floor) & 0.566 \\
Federated internal LoRA (Adam, backprop, averaged) & 0.648 \\
One-shot Gauss--Newton state & 0.666 \\
\textbf{Iterated Gauss--Newton (3 rounds)} & \textbf{0.710} \\
Centralized internal LoRA (Adam, backprop) & 0.755 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Iteration escapes the one-shot band with per-round linearization errors
$0.26\!\to\!0.14$ (measured, attached). A fixed $48$-coordinate mergeable
subspace closes to within $4.5$ points of $1{,}536$ freely-trained
parameters, and beats the federated version of the real thing. Honest
cost: Jacobian accumulation makes our method $\sim\!4\times$ more
expensive in cpu-s than SGD at this scale---the trade is compute for
exact mergeability.

\subsection{Incrementality: $O(\mathrm{new})$, zero forgetting, exact deletion}
Six periods of drifting data ($70\%$ class skew per period). The
incremental state (i) stays bit-identical to full retraining at every
period (max deviation $6\times10^{-11}$); (ii) cannot forget, while
warm-start SGD on new data collapses to $0.482$ vs $0.770$; (iii) keeps
per-period cost flat ($\times1.6$ over six periods) while replay
retraining grows $\times6.0$, for a cumulative measured advantage of
$3.4\times$ and diverging; and (iv) deletes a mid-stream contributor by
subtraction at $269\times$ less cost than replay-without-the-data,
exactly. No trajectory-based method in the comparison offers more than
two of these four properties at once.

\subsection{Continual inference accretion}
Feeding a repeated workload through the cascade, teacher escalation
declines $0.37\!\to\!0.13$ at quality $1.000$ (reference program;
$0.36\!\to\!0.30$ with quality $0.62\!\to\!0.86$ on real text), and
measured energy per accepted task falls $0.018\!\to\!0.007$\,J,
yielding $7.7\times$ verified outcomes per joule versus teacher-always.
A fleet-scale projection composing these measured micro-costs with a
declared workload model (labeled simulation) indicates $-81\%$ lifetime
energy versus teacher-always and $-51\%$ versus a tuned static cascade
over 24 months, with backbone upgrades absorbed by state migration
(below).

\subsection{Backbone migration}
Transporting the head state to an independently pretrained,
narrower backbone through a bridge fit on 600 public anchors recovers
$78\%$ of the cold-reset$\to$full-retrain gap
(reset $0.229$, transported $0.500$, retrain $0.578$;
bridge residual $0.603$ reported; \textsc{empirical-neural}).

\section{Limitations}
All results use small backbones pretrained on the task corpus; scale is
untested (though the mechanism should benefit from stronger
representations, which capture more of downstream adaptation linearly).
Evidence is classification/scoring-shaped; generative tasks are
untouched. Energy is measured CPU-time at declared wattage, not wall
power; the fleet projection is a simulation and labeled as such.
Migration is empirical. Byzantine-robust aggregation of validly signed
but malicious statistics is future work. Exactness claims hold only for
the declared frozen or linearized objectives---never for unrestricted
end-to-end training.

\section{Conclusion}
Replacing trained-adapter communication with exactly mergeable evidence
in a shared canonical basis turns distributed adaptation from an
optimization problem into an algebra: models become cheap materialized
views of a durable intelligence state that grows by addition, forgets
nothing, deletes exactly, iterates to near-backprop quality on internal
surfaces, and progressively transfers inference from large central
models to small edge models. The measured savings are one to two orders
of magnitude on the adaptation side and compounding on the serving side.
The decisive open test---identical machinery on large pretrained
backbones---requires only a representation extractor swap, which the
released code already supports.
